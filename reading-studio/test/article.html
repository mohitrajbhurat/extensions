The Quiet Art of Reading

By Ada Lovelace · March 12, 2024
Share on XShare on FacebookEmail this

Good typography disappears. When the measure is comfortable and the spacing is generous, the eye glides from line to line and the words do the work. Dr. Johnson knew this in 1755, and the lesson has not changed since.

We read about 250 words per minute, give or take. That rate collapses when a column is 1,200 pixels wide, because every return sweep becomes a small act of navigation. The fix is old and boring: a measure of roughly 60 to 75 characters.

Attention is a budget

Every blinking banner spends a little of it. A page that respects your attention spends almost none. This is why reader modes exist — they are a refusal, politely rendered in CSS.

Listening is reading, too

When a page is read aloud, sentence boundaries matter more than they look. "Did you know?" she asked. The answer took three sentences, e.g. this one, and the next, and one more besides.

The best interface is the one that gets out of the way.

  • Comfortable measure
  • Generous leading
  • Honest contrast

None of this is new. It is simply easy to forget, especially when a page has something to sell. So we rebuild the page, and read it in peace.
